Which method fits your goal?

Avalanche directs extra payments to the card with the highest APR, usually reducing total interest. Snowball directs extra payments to the card with the smallest outstanding balance, potentially eliminating an individual card sooner. Both methods continue making estimated minimum payments on other cards.

Worked example

Suppose you owe $4,500 at 25% APR and $2,800 at 19% APR, with a $350 monthly budget. The calculator models each card’s monthly interest and minimum payment, allocates the remaining budget to the selected target card, and rolls freed-up payment capacity toward remaining balances. Adjust the inputs to reflect your own statements.

Important limitations

Actual credit card interest commonly uses daily balance methods, not APR divided by 12. Introductory rates, penalty APRs, annual fees, cash advances, variable rates, payment dates, and purchases can change actual payoff costs. A fixed monthly budget may be insufficient to meet required minimum payments; the calculator flags that situation rather than assuming missed minimums are acceptable.

See the methodology and credit card guide for more detail. For issuer interest calculations, consult the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.
