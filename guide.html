Credit card planning guide

Understand APR versus actual interest

APR is an annualized rate. Many card issuers compute interest from a daily periodic rate applied to daily balances. A quick monthly model uses APR divided by 12, but the statement can differ when purchases, payments, fees, or balances change during a billing cycle. If your statement shows several APR categories, calculate each separately and consult the issuer’s allocation rules.

Why a fixed payment can outperform minimum-only payments

A fixed dollar payment keeps pressure on principal as the balance shrinks. A percentage-of-balance minimum tends to decrease with the balance, which can stretch repayment for years. An issuer’s real minimum formula may include a percentage, interest, fees, and a dollar floor. Our minimum-payment simulator is illustrative, not a statement prediction.

Balance transfers: calculate the fee before assuming savings

A 0% introductory offer may carry a transfer fee. On a $5,000 transfer with a 3% fee, the fee is $150. Whether the transfer saves money depends on the original APR, how quickly you pay, the promotional length, and the APR after the offer. New purchases on the same account may have different interest treatment.

Utilization is only one credit factor

Credit utilization is outstanding revolving balances divided by credit limits. It may be measured on individual accounts as well as in aggregate, and reporting dates matter. Lowering a reported balance can change the ratio, but no particular ratio guarantees a score or approval.

Grace periods and purchase interest

Some cards provide a grace period on new purchases if you pay the statement balance in full by the due date. Carrying a balance can affect grace-period treatment. Cash advances often have different rates and may begin accruing interest immediately. Check the card agreement.

Compare strategies without hiding assumptions

Use the same starting balance and payment budget across scenarios. Compare total interest, time to payoff, transfer fees, and whether you can pay the balance before an introductory offer expires. The cheapest mathematical result is not always the most manageable monthly payment.
